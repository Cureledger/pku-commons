Do not edit after capture.**

## The headline prediction — this one tests the model, not the city

The two-axis model claims Menu Fit and Accommodation are independent. Copenhagen's tasting-menu
density is the sharpest available test.

**Prediction:** starred Copenhagen tasting-menu restaurants will show **low menu fit** and **high
accommodation**, and the correlation between the two axes across the Copenhagen corpus will be
**negative or absent**.

**Reasoning:** a prepaid, ticketed, weeks-ahead booking means the kitchen knows exactly who is
coming, how many, and when; the menu is planned rather than reactive; and there is a booking
record with a contact field. Those are the best possible conditions for preparing something
different — the opposite of a walk-in at a busy à la carte restaurant.

**Falsified if:** the correlation comes out positive. In that case the two axes are measuring one
thing, and the model should be collapsed to a single scale rather than defended.

**Confound to control for:** price. Starred restaurants are expensive, and expense may drive
accommodation independently of format. A positive accommodation finding needs a price-matched à la
carte comparison before it is credited to the tasting-menu structure.

## Prior 5 is the trap prior

If Green Star venues turn out to be *worse* for PKU than average — plausible, since sustainable
cooking leans on legumes to displace meat — that is the single most useful negative finding this
project could produce, because it is exactly the inference a well-meaning user would make
unaided. Registering it now so the result is reportable either way.
