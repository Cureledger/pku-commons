# European PKU Guidelines, First Revision (2025) — summary

**Citation.** van Wegberg AMJ, MacDonald A, Ahring K, et al. *European guidelines on diagnosis and treatment of phenylketonuria: First revision.* Mol Genet Metab 145 (2025) 109125. DOI 10.1016/j.ymgme.2025.109125. CC-BY 4.0. PMID 40378670.

**Document.** 21 authors across 4 working groups; GRADE methodology; literature reviewed to September 2022; 87 recommendations (70 updated from the 2017 edition + 20 new topics); each statement required ≥75% panel agreement. Where a recommendation was not amended, the 2017 text remains in force. Companion documents: 2017 complete guidelines (Orphanet JRD 12:162) and the 2020 PKU dietary handbook (Orphanet JRD 15:171).

---

## Diagnosis and classification (§4–5)
- Differential diagnosis of all hyperphenylalaninemia must exclude BH4-metabolism defects and DNAJC12 deficiency (pterins + dihydropteridine reductase in DBS/urine; DNAJC12 genotyping when those are normal and PAH genotyping is not conclusive) (Rec 4). Patient genotyping is recommended — needed to distinguish PAH from DNAJC12 deficiency and helps predict sapropterin responsiveness (Rec 5).
- Classification is now by treatment need, not historical severity (Rec 6): (a) not requiring treatment (Phe <360 µmol/L), (b) requiring treatment, cofactor-responsive, (c) requiring treatment, cofactor-unresponsive.
- Treatment should start as soon as possible, ideally before 10 days of age (Rec 7). No treatment when untreated Phe <360 µmol/L, with monitoring until at least age 6 (Rec 8); treat all children with untreated Phe ≥360 µmol/L (Rec 9).

## Blood Phe treatment targets (§6, µmol/L)

| Group | Target | Rec | Evidence / strength | Panel agreement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Children ≤12 y | 120–360 | 10 | moderate (B) / strong | 100% |
| Adolescents 12–18 y | 120–600 | 11 | low (C) / strong | 100% |
| Adults >18 y | 120–600 | 13 | very low (D) / conditional | 75% (5% disagree) |
| Pregnancy / maternal PKU | 120–360 | 15 | moderate (B) / strong | 100% |

- Children's and pregnancy targets (120–360) are unchanged from 2017 and rest on the strongest evidence. The adult upper bound (600) is the weakest statement in the document — conditional, very-low evidence, and more caution is now attached to it than in 2017.
- Women with Phe >360 µmol/L should reach target pre-conception; if already pregnant, reach target within 7 days (Rec 16).
- Lifelong follow-up at a specialist metabolic centre is recommended for all patients, including untreated adults (Rec 12).

## Monitoring (§7)
- **Biomarker:** blood Phe (DBS and/or plasma) is the single recommended biomarker (Rec 17). Phe variation/fluctuation and the Phe/Tyr ratio are *not* recommended for routine care — no agreed target range exists (Rec 19).
- **DBS vs plasma are not interchangeable:** plasma reported from 15% lower to 30% higher than DBS; tandem MS/MS vs ion-exchange chromatography usually differ <10% (§7.1.1). DBS by MS/MS is endorsed for monitoring only with assured sample quality (patient training) and lab accreditation (Rec 18).
- **Testing frequency (good control):** infants 0–1 y weekly; ages 1–12 y fortnightly; adolescents and adults monthly; pregnancy ≥twice weekly (weekly pre-conception). Clinic dietary assessment = 3-day food record / 24-h recall; plasma amino acids annually (Table 2).
- Neurocognitive testing offered ~5 y, ~12 y, ~18 y then every 5–10 y. Routine neuroimaging (Rec 30) and routine eye/heart/kidney follow-up (Rec 31) are not recommended absent clinical indication. BMD by DXA first measured in early adolescence (Rec 34). Annual nutritional review for anyone on a Phe-restricted or self-restricted diet (Recs 35–36).

## Dietary treatment (§8)
- **Phe tolerance** = the amount of Phe (mg/kg/day or mg/day) that keeps blood Phe in range; titrated methodically and reassessed annually, with a Phe challenge considered if consistently in range to avoid over-restriction (Rec 39). Natural-protein tolerance from age 3 is typically <0.5 g/kg/day on diet alone.
- **Protein requirement:** at least the WHO/FAO/UNU 2007 safe level, plus additional protein equivalent from substitutes — +40% for children ≤17 y; +20–40% for adults (Rec 40).
- **Protein substitutes** (foods for special medical purposes): Phe-free/low-Phe, given ≥3 times/day (Rec 41). cGMP may be used from age ≥4 y provided its residual Phe is counted in the daily allocation and its amino acid profile meets age requirements (Rec 42); most formulations contain ~36 mg Phe per 20 g protein equivalent. Routine tyrosine supplementation is not advised — already added to substitutes (Rec 43). LNAA not recommended for routine use; not in children or pregnancy (Rec 44).
- **Free foods:** fruits and vegetables with Phe ≤75 mg/100 g may be eaten without measurement; those ≥76 mg/100 g must be weighed and counted; potatoes are excluded (variable Phe) (Rec 46). The allowed-without-measurement list itself is in Table 3 of the 2020 handbook, not this document. Special low-protein foods are an important energy source but should not exceed normal energy/fat/sugar/salt limits (Rec 47).
- **Aspartame (E951)** hydrolyses ~50% to free Phe and should be avoided; content is often undisclosed and varies by brand/country. Neotame is safe (peptide bond resists hydrolysis) (Rec 49).
- Breastfeeding combined with Phe-free infant amino acid formula is encouraged (Rec 48). Additional sections cover dental care, disordered eating (higher eating-disorder prevalence than general population), overweight, exercise, burden of diet, and education. Education notes that "PKU mobile apps have an increasing role" (§8.14, Rec 56).

## Special groups and pharmacotherapy (§9–10)
- **Maternal PKU:** detailed diet, energy, weight, tyrosine, folic acid, DHA/EPA guidance; pregnancy target 120–360 µmol/L with ≥twice-weekly monitoring (§9.1).
- **Sapropterin:** for identified responders; guidance on responder identification, diet/follow-up, and pregnancy (§10.1).
- **Pegvaliase:** for patients >16 y not achieving control with sapropterin (Rec 86, high evidence); goal is ≥0.8 g/kg/day protein without substitutes while avoiding hypophenylalaninemia (Rec 87); not currently recommended in pregnancy/pre-conception (§10.2).
- **Emerging therapies:** gene therapy (in trials), sepiapterin (Phase II), renal Phe-reabsorption inhibition, other chaperones (§10.3).

## Future research and patient perspective (§11–12)
- Stated gaps: optimal lifetime Phe targets (esp. the adult upper bound), home monitoring of blood Phe, improving home-measurement accuracy, and the difficulty of building large PKU databases without multi-centre collaboration.
- The ESPKU patient section identifies the lack of consensus on adult upper Phe thresholds as the central unresolved issue, notes ACMG/USA guidance is stricter, and calls for research beyond Phe levels (aging, mental health, menopause, multimorbidity).
- **Evidence caveat stated by the authors:** many recommendations are graded low or very low and rest partly on consensus rather than data.
